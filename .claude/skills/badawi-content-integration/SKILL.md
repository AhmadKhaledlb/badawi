# BADAWI Content Integration

## Purpose

Move already-approved BADAWI curriculum, cultural, safety, and challenge content into the application while preserving its structure, substantive meaning, provenance, verification state, safety state, cultural review state, and approved competency relationships. This skill integrates content — it does not create it. Writing new challenge text, safety guidance, or cultural material is out of scope here regardless of how small or "obviously fine" it looks; see "Content safety boundaries."

## Governance

This skill is subordinate to `CLAUDE.md`, the locked Phase 1–4 specifications, and approved Phase 5 decisions (`docs/decisions/`). It grants no new authority over content, curriculum, safety, or cultural representation — those remain owned elsewhere. If anything here appears to conflict with those sources, they win; stop and surface the conflict.

## When to use / when not to

Use for anything that touches content data or its pipeline: the hierarchy entities themselves, competency mappings, prerequisites/ordering, challenge/preparation/reflection content, safety or cultural content, content metadata (provenance, verification, review state), content schemas, or validation/import/transformation logic.

Don't force this workflow onto work that merely references a content entity without touching its data or structure — e.g., a UI bug fix that happens to render a Challenge title but doesn't change what content exists, how it's structured, or what it means.

## Required invariants

Every integration must preserve:

- **Hierarchy**: Region → Environment → Pack → Unit → Challenge
- **V1 scope**: Arabian Peninsula → Desert → Desert Foundations → 6 Units → 28 Challenges
- **Learning arc**: READ → UNDERSTAND → PREPARE → ORIENT → MOVE → INTEGRATE
- **Competency loop**: OBSERVE → REASON → ACT → ADAPT → REFLECT, with SAFETY surrounding the loop
- **Competency levels**: Aware → Capable → Independent → Adaptive — there is no Expert level

These aren't arbitrary structural rules — they encode the actual pedagogical and safety model BADAWI is built on. Restructuring them, even to make a schema tidier or an import script simpler, changes what BADAWI teaches and how it evaluates safety, which is why it requires approval rather than engineering judgment.

## Workflow

### 1. Identify source

Determine where the content actually came from and whether it's approved for integration. Content existing in the repository, looking complete, or having been generated by AI is not evidence of approval — none of those are the same thing as having gone through the content/research/safety/cultural review it needs. If the source or approval status is unclear, that's a fact to report, not a gap to close by assuming the best case.

### 2. Determine trust state

Identify and preserve, where applicable:
- provenance/source
- verification state
- research status
- safety status
- cultural review status
- version
- uncertainty

Missing information stays missing — carry it forward as missing rather than filling it in with a plausible default. Never invent or infer approval that isn't actually there; a content item's trust state should reflect what's actually known about it, not what would be convenient for it to be.

### 3. Validate structure

Check what's applicable to the content being integrated:
- hierarchy placement
- identifiers
- relationships
- ordering
- prerequisites
- competency mappings
- required metadata
- schema conformity
- safety metadata
- provenance/review metadata

**If the required content schema doesn't exist yet, don't invent a material schema autonomously.** A content schema is a persistent data model — `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/architecture/README.md` both treat schema changes as requiring approval, and content schemas are no exception just because they hold curriculum data rather than user data. Escalate instead.

### 4. Integrate

Transform approved content into the required technical representation without changing its substantive meaning. Permitted transformations: serialization, field mapping, formatting, technical normalization, schema-conforming representation. Substantive rewriting — changing what the content actually says, teaches, or claims — requires the approval that content changes need; a transformation step is not the place for editorial judgment to sneak in.

### 5. Validate result

After integrating, check that no content was:
- lost
- unintentionally duplicated
- improperly reordered
- silently rewritten
- mapped to the wrong hierarchy position or competency
- promoted to a higher trust state than it actually has
- detached from its provenance
- stripped of safety/cultural-review metadata where required

A transformation that's structurally correct but drops or upgrades trust metadata along the way has failed just as much as one that corrupts the content itself — the metadata is what lets everyone downstream know how much to trust what they're looking at.

## Content safety boundaries

Never, under this skill:
- fabricate challenge content
- invent sources or citations
- generate authoritative survival or field guidance from model knowledge
- invent traditional practices
- infer unsupported tribal/community attribution
- present culturally synthetic material as authentic
- mark content verified because Claude generated, formatted, transformed, or processed it
- weaken safety meaning for layout or convenience
- convert stopping/modifying/postponing/refusing into automatic failure
- add an Expert competency level
- replace BADAWI competency with generic numeric mastery scoring
- restructure the 6 Units / 28 Challenges without required approval

Clearly marked development placeholders are fine when approved content isn't available yet — e.g. `"TODO: approved hydration guidance required"` is acceptable development content. Inventing plausible-sounding hydration guidance to fill the same slot is not, regardless of how reasonable it reads. There's no size or triviality threshold below which this stops applying — a one-line placeholder gap and a whole missing section carry the same rule.

## Readiness

Keep these three states distinct and don't let technical integration promote any of the other two:
1. **Engineering readiness** — the content is correctly structured and integrated
2. **Content/research readiness** — the content itself has been researched and verified
3. **Safety/cultural approval** — the content has cleared safety and cultural review

A successful integration only ever establishes (1). Report the other two honestly as whatever state they're actually in, even when that's "unknown" or "not yet reviewed."

## Interactions with other skills

- When content integration affects an approved UI surface, `badawi-screen-implementation` may be relevant alongside this one.
- When the content affects Field Mode or safety-critical field behavior, `badawi-field-mode` must also be considered — don't treat content-schema correctness as a substitute for that skill's safety checks.
- `badawi-feature-verification` provides the final BADAWI-specific verification gate; this skill's own validation steps don't replace it.

All of these, including this skill, remain subordinate to `CLAUDE.md` and authoritative BADAWI documentation.

## Output

Report:
- **Content integrated** — what was moved in and where it landed in the hierarchy
- **Source** — where it came from, and its approval status for integration
- **Structure validated** — which checks from "Validate structure" were run
- **Trust/review states preserved** — provenance, verification, safety, and cultural-review state for the content touched
- **Transformations performed** — exactly what technical transformation was applied
- **Validation results** — outcome of the "Validate result" checks
- **Missing information** — anything that was missing and stayed missing rather than being filled in
- **Outstanding content/research review** — what's still needed before content/research readiness
- **Outstanding safety/cultural approval** — what's still needed before that approval
- **Any escalation required** — per the invariants and boundaries above
